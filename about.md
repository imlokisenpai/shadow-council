**Shadow council** is a small, leaderless collective. There is no president, no
spokesperson, no membership fee and no position that anyone else in the room is
obliged to hold. Three handles are listed on the
[names page]({{ '/members/' | relative_url }}), and they are the entire
organisation chart — but do the sum before you trust it. There is **one person**,
writing under two of those handles, and one machine that assembles the site
rather than a person who writes. We would rather you hear that here than infer
it later.

We write about power: what the constitution promises, what the courts actually
decided, and what happens in the gap between the two. We are based in India. We
write in English. We are not a party, not a campaign, not a NGO and not a
substitute for either.

## What lands here

- **The record.** Named cases, named statutes, dated. If we assert something
  falsifiable, there is a document behind it and a link to it.
- **The gap.** Where a right is written down and then administered around.
  This is most of what we write about, because it is where the interesting
  thing happens.
- **The analysis.** Why a crowd does what it does, and what institutions do
  with crowds once they are done. No metaphysics, no grievance, no enemies.
- **The odd piece.** Occasionally a poem, because a feeling is a piece of
  evidence about a person and pretending otherwise is a form of lying.

## The rules we hold ourselves to

They are short and they are on the [names page]({{ '/members/' | relative_url }}) in full. The three
that matter most:

1. **No one speaks for anyone else.** Every line is attributable to whoever
   wrote it.
2. **Sources or it does not go up.** A claim without a document is a rumour
   wearing our name.
3. **Corrections go up where the error went up.** Not in a footnote, not quietly
   edited, not in a changelog nobody reads.

## How to disagree with us

Better than you think, and the method is boring on purpose. If we have got
something wrong, open an issue on the repository with the source that proves
it. If a source we cited turns out to say something different from what we said
it said, that is the strongest possible version of the argument and you should
lead with it.

We do not moderate disagreement. The only thing we will remove is a doxxing
attempt, and we will tell you when we have.

## Colophon

Static Jekyll, no trackers, no cookies, no analytics, no third-party fonts,
nothing server-side. The background is a `<canvas>` drawing falling glyphs in
your browser which then forgets about it. The glitch is `clip-path` and two
pseudo-elements. No image assets. Source is public, the build runs on GitHub
Actions, and if a page looks wrong it is almost certainly the CSS.

Comments are off. That is a decision, not an oversight — the issues on the
repo are a better channel than a comment box, because they are searchable and
because a comment box is somewhere to perform rather than somewhere to
disprove something.
