Three of us have been talking for long enough that somebody eventually said the
quiet part out loud: a group with a leader is a group that can be shut down by
removing one person. Not quickly, and not cheaply — but shut down, because the
leader is both the target and the only place the members' leverage is
concentrated.

So we did the obvious thing, which is to not have one.

Three handles, listed in full on the [names page]({{ '/members/' | relative_url }}).
Two of them are people. The third is a language model that assembles this site:
it runs the repository, the templates, the feed and the deploy, and it types the
words into the places they belong. It holds no position and it decides nothing
about what gets published. We are naming it in the first paragraph of the
founding document rather than in a colophon because a collective built on "no
spokesperson" and "receipts" has no business having a machine do a material
share of its talking quietly. Readers are entitled to discount everything here
by whatever weight they think a machine's typing deserves, and they should
discount the prose most heavily. The sources do not get discounted, and those
are the parts you should be checking.

## The structure

There is no president. There is no spokesperson. There is no secretary, no
treasurer, no office, and no person whose agreement turns a draft into a
position. A thing gets published because the people who wrote it are willing to
put their handle on it, and if that is one of us or all three, the number does
not change the standard.

This has an obvious weakness and we should name it first rather than let you
find it: **a structure with no leader has nobody to answer for it.** When we
publish something false, there is no person to be embarrassed into retracting
it, no head that can be pressured, no position that must be defended. The
integrity of this place rests entirely on the willingness of the people in it
to be boring and to correct themselves. That is a weak guarantee. It is the
only guarantee we could find that does not involve building the off switch
ourselves.

## The rules

There are five. They are in full on the [names page]({{ '/members/' | relative_url }}).

**No spokesperson.** If you want to know what we think, you will have to read
it and then ask us whether we meant it. That is a worse system than a phone
number and it is the whole point.

**No line.** None of us holds a position that outlives their agreement to it.
We are not a party, and we are not going to acquire a manifesto that we then
have to defend for the next thirty years. Where we agree we will say so
plainly. Where we do not, you will be able to see that too.

**Receipts.** A claim without a document attached to it is a rumour wearing our
name. This is the rule that does the most work and the one we will be judged
on. If we tell you a commission found something, we tell you *which* commission
and *when it reported*. If we tell you a statute exists, we give you the
section. If we are reasoning rather than reporting, we say so in the sentence,
not in a hedge at the end.

**Correction in place.** When we get something wrong, the fix goes up where the
error went up — same page, visible, not quietly edited into the git history and
never mentioned. If a correction is buried we will have committed the worst
possible offence, which is being wrong *and* concealing it.

**No performance.** Nobody here is auditioning. We do not post because a
sentence gets engagement, and the tell for that is writing to be quoted rather
than writing to be checked. We are aware this makes us boring. Boring is the
point.

## What we are not

Not a party. Not a campaign. Not an NGO. Not a replacement for any of those
things, and not a staging post for them either. Not a party *organisation* in
the sense that matters to the law — the distinction is legal, it is narrow, and
we are not interested in the grey area around it.

We are also not neutral, and we should stop pretending that word means anything.
Everyone writing here has a position on what a government is allowed to do to
the people it governs. What we are committed to is that the position has to
survive contact with the primary source, that the source has to be one we have
actually read, and that our reasons have to be on the page where somebody can
argue with them.

## How to check us

Everything here is in a public repository. Every claim that can be checked can
be checked against the primary document rather than against our summary of it,
and the summaries are the part most likely to be wrong. That is intentional: if
you have to take our word for the contents of a judgment, we have built the
wrong kind of site.

Disagreement goes in the issues, with the source. We do not moderate it. The one
thing we will take down is an attempt to identify one of us, and when we do we
will say that we did.

open an issue and say why, with a source.
